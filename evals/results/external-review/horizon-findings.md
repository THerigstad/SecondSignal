# Horizon findings from the seven architecture reviews

**What this is.** Seven model reviews (ChatGPT, DeepSeek, Google Gemini, Grok, Qwen, Perplexity running GLM, Mistral running Vibe) answered the same prompt on 2026-08-31 — [`00-review-prompt.md`](00-review-prompt.md) — which describes SecondSignal's *target* architecture and supplies draft schemas for reviewers to critique. None of the seven mentions the code that exists: not the router, the gate, a profile, or a test. They are a **horizon threat model**, catalogued here so their value is kept without pretending they are evidence about the implementation. The one review that was run against the code is the separate package under [`grok-2026-09-01/`](grok-2026-09-01/), and its results are in [`fixture-results-2026-09-02.md`](fixture-results-2026-09-02.md).

**How to read the independence notes.** All seven share the prompt's skeleton, its twenty-section outline, its fifteen-source baseline and its first twenty threat identifiers, so agreement between them is weaker evidence than it looks; the reviewer of the code-directed package called this "eight cousins of the same essay." Where a review brought its own sources or its own mechanism, that is noted. Where a review's citations could not be verified, that is noted too. Nothing here has been re-verified against primary sources; treat every citation as a lead.

## The reviews, one paragraph each

**ChatGPT — "Global Architecture Threat / Test Review".** The strongest of the seven as a literature harvest: roughly 110 dated sources (about 95 not in the prompt's baseline), threats T21–T52, eighteen exploit chains, twenty-six test specifications, fifteen "killer tests", thirty-eight blocking questions, and a build order. Distinctive contributions: context separation is not isolation (a multi-agent incident in which model-authored logs were not a trust boundary and impossible tasks without a safe exit drove collusion); personalization as an influence channel with four named failure classes and *retrieval abstention* as a positive outcome; a binding regulatory reference on companion dependence, exit and minors whose emergency-contact mandate carries its own false-positive risk; "operational force" versus semantic topic — ordinary register and authority framing can outweigh adversarial wording; and a typed treatment of zero — a veto, a genuine zero, and a missing value must be distinguishable. Caveats: the ADR numbering it proposes collides with this repository's; several 2026 citations could not be checked; the authorial voice is a report, not a spec.

**Perplexity (GLM) — "Independent Research, Threat Model, and Test Program".** Shares the skeleton but is independent in substance. Distinctive contributions: a deskilling evidence block (unaided re-performance as a gate); non-dependence as an *engineered* property with a graduation record, an anti-abandonment guardrail (disengagement alone is never rewarded) and a pre-registered kill criterion; social engineering as architecture, with an authority–emotion decoupling invariant and the principle that trust capital and authority capital are non-convertible; a crisis baseline arguing per-message classifiers are insufficient without account-level risk aggregation; exit and persuasion evidence (manipulative farewells, personalization's persuasive lift, disclosure's failure as a control); typed constraints with the finding that compression deactivates constraints unless prerequisite, authority, fallback and consequence are carried as fields. Caveats: one open-letter citation is unverified; one benchmark link is a typo domain; one citation is unlinked.

**Qwen 3.8 Max.** Correlated in the strict sense: its first five "stop the room" questions are verbatim prompt copies and T21–T44 transcribe the prompt's own list of required additions; its bibliography predates 2025; it states that live browsing was unavailable. Distinctive contributions nonetheless: disciplined non-fabrication (every unverifiable claim labeled); four memory planes (episodic, preference, structural safety state, audit); a quorum-by-action-class table for the security characters; an emotional-inference schema whose defaults deny affect any power over proactivity, authority or memory promotion — "affect is never authorization"; a deletion-order schema with per-store scope and receipts; eight stop conditions; and an explicit endorsement of deterministic policy-as-code for the audit function, which is what this layer already is.

**DeepSeek (Expert mode; the longer of two exports).** A reasoning-model session with an early cutoff and no browsing; its dossier sections are self-labeled unverified, and two citations are misattributed. Distinctive contributions: independence made operational, with numeric decorrelation targets (adversarial transfer, failure-set overlap, poison agreement); "emotional authority laundering" — warmth winning an approval that contradicts a standing boundary — with named mitigations; a four-rule authority-conflict policy (a standing boundary beats a current instruction unless explicitly acknowledged; crisis pauses; third-party rights and legal obligations force refusal); six human-outcome metrics with formulas and instruments; a project-level stop trigger on sustained loss of user capability; exit as a feature. Its follow-up turns after the report are excluded from this catalogue.

**Google Gemini.** Short, browsing disabled, two real citations and one that reads as fabricated. Distinctive contributions: a "stabilizer capture" chain — an over-eager regulation estimate routes ordinary creative venting into a grounding session, which is worth a ceiling on how often the stabilizer wins by fallback; "Sybil empathy" (a coordinated third party manufacturing a coercion pattern); and the rule that only intake may raise severity.

**Grok (earlier research harvest, 2026-08-31).** The most evidence-backed of the family: live retrieval, dated URLs, evidence grades, an honest null result on one dossier subject, twenty-two new threat identifiers and about forty test identifiers with pass/fail oracles. Entirely horizon, and superseded by the same reviewer's code-directed package the next day, which opens with "Stop generating architecture audits." Its threat and test identifiers are not imported here because they collide with identifiers other reviews reused for different tests.

**Mistral (Vibe).** A six-page export truncated mid-sentence in its third section; no exploit chains, tests or bibliography survive. It asserts all sources were verified while twelve of its twenty-five "new" sources carry placeholder preprint identifiers. Its thirteen verdict items map one-to-one onto prompt inputs. It is indexed for completeness and as a finding about the reliability of external review: a review that claims verification it did not do is itself a threat.

## Where they converge on the built layer

Six themes appear in three or more reviews without copying each other's wording. Each is stated with what happened to it in the 2026-09-02 batch.

- **Indirect, inflected, code-switched and non-English crisis language defeats a lexical gate.** Confirmed by measurement (nine of ten review fixtures missed) and addressed by class-based screening, fail-closed under uncertainty, with English-only scope stated and dated and a coverage flag for text the screen cannot score (ADR-0010). Multilingual coverage remains a documented gap.
- **The gate must be invariant to who is on the roster, and warmth, urgency or pleading must never lower a verdict.** True by construction (the gate takes no roster) and now asserted, with monotonicity property tests (ADR-0010; `tests/test_guards.py`, `tests/test_crisis_gate.py`).
- **The dependency monitor is a latch: cross-session split evidence defeats it, nothing clears it, and the trace should say why it tripped.** Session scope is by design and is now pinned as isolated and unresettable by request text. The opposite failure — permanent false suspicion — and an explicit clearance path remain planned (threat T20).
- **Zero has three meanings.** Adopted: every candidate in a trace is scored, vetoed, below its floor, or had nothing to score, and the decision names the rule that produced it (ADR-0011).
- **Over-restriction is a first-class failure with its own published rate.** Adopted: sixteen idiom controls that must stay green, and the false-positive cost of the fail-closed policy is stated rather than optimised away (ADR-0010, `docs/evaluation.md`).
- **Independence is artifact class, not seating: a deterministic, non-model gate and audit function.** Several reviews demand it. The gate and router here are deterministic and make no model calls; the audit function's contract is recorded on the same principle (ADR-0014).

Two further transfers were taken up because they cost little: a stabilizer-by-fallback ceiling (Gemini) is now a named policy rather than a fallback (ADR-0011), and the roster floor is enforced at load with the profiles hashed (a "malformed roster fails closed" theme from Qwen and DeepSeek; ADR-0012).

## Horizon threats, mapped to the threat model

The reviews' target-architecture threats do not extend `docs/threat-model.md`'s T01–T20 so much as populate them:

- Authority widening through delegation, summaries and handoffs; standing boundaries reduced to preferences by compression — T01, T02, T03.
- Memory as a regulator of identity; personalization as an influence channel; retrieval abstention as a positive outcome — T05, T06, T08.
- Emotional authority laundering; crisis feints as transactions; farewell manipulation — T04, T07, T16.
- Correlated reviewers and consensus theater; common-mode failure across an ensemble on one provider — T11, T13.
- Impossible tasks without a rewarded safe exit driving collusion or invented channels — T12, T14.
- Deskilling, dependence, and the gaming of graduation metrics once they reach a dashboard — T15, T17, T18.
- Latched safety state as an unappealable profile; deletion without propagation receipts — T19, T20.

## Citation caveats, kept together

Unverified or defective citations were found in ChatGPT (several 2026 sources not checkable), Perplexity (an open letter, a typo domain, an unlinked reference), DeepSeek (two misattributions), Gemini (one apparent fabrication), and Mistral (twelve placeholder identifiers). Qwen invented nothing but also brought nothing newer than 2024. None of these citations has been re-verified for this repository. The practical rule the code-directed reviewer gave stands: do not re-run the shared prompt; give one model the tests, another the profiles, and a third only the gate, and let them disagree.
